# Abstract 

Large datasets and high capacity models have accelerated progress in vision and language. This work introduces a platform aimed at bringing comparable gains to embodied learning, world models, and robotics. We present EnvDreamer, a framework that uses large language and vision language models to generate Unreal Engine 5 environments for embodied AI and robot training. EnvDreamer enables sampling of large, diverse, interactive, customizable, and validator passed virtual environments for training and evaluation across navigation, interaction, and ma-nipulation. We illustrate the platform with a large set of generated scenes and simple baselines. Policies trained on EnvDreamer generated environments, without explicit mapping or human task supervision, achieve competitive results on multi-ple embodied benchmarks spanning navigation, rearrangement, and manipulation. EnvDreamer also supports image-conditioned reconstruction for real-to-sim studies. Finally, we release EnvDreamer-20k, a dataset of 20,000 validator passed envi-ronments with task programs, scene graphs, trajectories, and metadata to support reproducible benchmarking. 

# 1 Introduction 

Large scale training on diverse data has accelerated progress in vision and language. In contrast, embodied AI and world model research still rely on simulators with far fewer scenes and limited task diversity, which makes it difficult to study long horizon behavior and robust generalization. Although recent simulators have improved physics, manipulation, and sensor realism, scaling interactive environments to tens or hundreds of thousands of scenes while preserving quality and reproducibility remains challenging Shen et al. [2021] Ehsani et al. [2021]. Pipelines based on manual authoring or 3D scans are expensive to expand by orders of magnitude and are not designed for language first task authoring or rapid iteration Chang et al. [2017] Dai et al. [2017]. We present EnvDreamer (Figure 1), a framework that generates fully interactive, physics enabled Unreal Engine 5 environments for embodied AI, world models, and robotics. Given a natural language description and optionally images, EnvDreamer plans assets, layout, and task specifications; compiles these plans to Unreal Python; and iteratively revises scenes and task logic using simulator feedback with unit style validators that check playability, physics consistency, and goal completeness. A large asset library with semantic categories, materials, and articulated parts is used to populate scenes automatically while enforcing physical plausibility and commonsense constraints. Lighting, textures, and materials can vary on load to increase diversity and reduce overfitting. Beyond text to scene synthesis Tan et al. [2019] Nauata et al. [2020] Wu et al. [2019] Wang et al. [2021], EnvDreamer supports image conditioned generation for sim to real studies, allowing researchers to reconstruct target environments in simulation for training and evaluation.

showing competitive results for world model pretraining and embodied policy learning with analyses of zero shot and few shot transfer relative to procedural and non interactive baselines.

Scene generation and editing. Work on generating indoor environments spans text-to-scene pipelines, floorplan synthesis, and procedural layout methods. Recent agentic systems such as SceneSmith Anonymous [2026] hierarchically compose simulation-ready indoor scenes from natural language through designer–critic–orchestrator agent interactions over stages of architectural layout, furniture, and small-object placement, combining text-to-3D asset synthesis with physics-based feasibility post-processing to produce dense, statically stable rooms with explicit physical properties (mass, friction, inertia, articulation) for direct robotics simulation. EnvDreamer takes a complemen-tary stance focused on corpus-scale embodied training rather than per-scene realism: scenes are interactive and physics enabled, placements obey commonsense and physical constraints, appearance and lighting can vary on load, and tasks compile to executable programs that are verified by validators before inclusion in the released dataset, supporting downstream world-model and embodied policy pretraining. 

Robotics simulators and sim to real. For robot learning, simulators place greater weight on sensor models, accurate dynamics, and integration with control stacks. Isaac Sim NVIDIA [2023] provides high fidelity physics, photoreal rendering, and native links to robotics middleware, which makes it well suited for manipulation and perception studies, though scene construction is often manual and not language first. RLBench James et al. [2020], RoboSuite Zhu et al. [2020], and Sapien Xiang et al. [2020] focus on fine grained manipulation skills and benchmark tasks with detailed kinematics and contact dynamics. EnvDreamer is complementary: it provides language driven scene and task synthesis in Unreal Engine 5 with validator checks and exposes APIs for navigation and manipulation so that policies trained in EnvDreamer can be transferred to real hardware, with image conditioned reconstruction available for sim to real studies when needed. 

# 3 EnvDreamer 

EnvDreamer is a framework for language driven generation of interactive Unreal Engine 5 Epic Games [2024] environments. It integrates large language and vision language models Radford et al. [2021] OpenAI [2023], including a GPT-5–class vLLM backend for interactive editing, with the UE5 Remote Control and Python interfaces so that scenes can be authored, edited, and validated at scale. Figure 3 illustrates an interactive web-session setup in which a vLLM-based smart agent receives user prompts and issues Remote Control commands to modify a running UE5 environment. As in hand authored UE5 levels, environments produced by EnvDreamer support navigation, manipulation, and multi agent interaction with accurate physics and rich materials. 

Overview. Given a specification in natural language or a small set of images, EnvDreamer plans assets, layout, and task logic, compiles the plan to Unreal Python, applies changes through Remote Control, and returns renders and a compact scene graph. A validator suite checks playability, physics 4Figure 4: Interactive scene in EnvDreamer. Ego-centric view from the embodied agent with annotated object relations ( on, under, between, near, inside ) and a bird’s-eye view of the procedurally generated multi-room layout. The robot is rendered in-scene, and objects are spawned with realistic and diverse geometry, materials, and placements that afford navigation and manipulation. consistency, and goal completeness before a scene or task is accepted. The system exposes a web interface for authoring and review and supports rapid iteration. 

Plan–compile–apply–validate loop. Figure 1 illustrates the generation loop. A planner proposes either a scene program (assets, placements, relations) or a layout description (rooms, bounds, cameras, materials). A normalizer aligns proposals to a lightweight mirror of the scene graph. A compiler emits Unreal Python for immediate edits and falls back to C++ only when custom rules, sensors, or physics components are required. An executor applies actions via Remote Control, captures renders and a scene graph diff, and the validators assess reachability, collisions, articulation constraints, and task predicates. The loop supports two modes: direct build , which applies edits live to the running level, and layout build , which materializes reusable layouts into scenes. 

Diversity. EnvDreamer produces diversity across multiple facets relevant to embodied evaluation. 

Layouts: from single rooms to multi room houses and outdoor lots, with variable room counts, aspect ratios, and connectivity patterns (Figure 2). Assets: a large library of household, office, and outdoor objects with articulated parts and semantic categories; placement rules reflect commonsense room and location preferences. Materials and lighting: materials, textures, and lighting parameters vary across loads to simulate time of day and appearance changes. Behaviors and tasks: tasks range from object search and rearrangement to tool use and multi step manipulation, encoded as executable programs with explicit preconditions and success metrics Yu et al. [2019]. 

Interactivity. Objects in EnvDreamer environments support comprehensive physical interactions. Items can be grasped, placed, opened, closed, toggled between states, or articulated through their joints. UE5’s physics engine enforces realistic constraints, collisions, and dynamics, enabling complex behaviours such as stacking, tool use, and multi-step manipulation. Appliances have functional states (e.g., ovens that open and heat), containers can be filled or emptied, and furniture can be rearranged. Scenes are authored to preserve navigable free space while maximizing interaction opportunities, with objects placed at reachable heights and orientations that afford natural manipulation. This enables long-horizon tasks requiring sequential interactions with multiple objects capabilities that static scan-based environments cannot provide. 

Customization. Scene specifications admit simple controls over room counts, categories present or absent, clutter level, lighting ranges, articulation availability, and task families. Small prompt changes or parameter sweeps yield families of related scenes, facilitating controlled generalization tests and curriculum-style progression over layouts and object configurations, as exemplified by the variations in Fig. 4. 5Figure 5: Task-conditioned dataset generation pipeline for EnvDreamer–20k. A user specifies a scenario by providing a prompt and a high-resolution viewport screenshot plus the current environment JSON description (left). A language model decomposes the request into a sequence of grounded sub-tasks such as “Walk to the kitchen” or “Put pizza on the plate” (middle). The system then checks which preconditions are satisfied by comparing object relations and automatically adding missing objects required to solve each task (e.g., placing a plate on the table). Finally, the validated environment details and the set of available tasks are exported as JSON and rendered through the EnvDreamer API to produce entries in the EnvDreamer–20k dataset (right). 

Scale and efficiency. EnvDreamer represents scenes as compact JSON-like programs with de-terministic seeds, enabling both static datasets and on-the-fly generation during training. The plan–compile–apply–validate loop is fully automatic and returns short iterations suitable for large-scale sampling. Scenes load directly in UE5 at training time, keeping storage overhead modest while maintaining high frame rates. 

Embodied robots. EnvDreamer ships with ready-to-use robot configurations for manipulation and locomotion; Fig. 4 shows one humanoid agent in a bedroom configuration. We include a Franka arm for pick-and-place, tool use, and contact-rich tasks, and a Unitree Go1 for exploration, navigation, and retrieval. Policies can access navigation and manipulation interfaces, RGB and depth observations, semantic and instance masks, and domain randomization over textures, lighting, clutter, and physics. 

Image to environment for sim to real. For sim to real studies, EnvDreamer supports image conditioned generation. Given photographs of a target space, a vision language model proposes a scene graph aligned to the images, and EnvDreamer instantiates semantically matched assets in UE5. This capability enables reconstruction of target environments in simulation with minimal manual authoring. 

# 4 EnvDreamer–20k 

We demonstrate the utility of our platform with a sampled set of 20,000 fully interactive environments produced by the generation loop in Section 3, which we refer to as EnvDreamer–20k . Figure 5 summarizes the task-conditioned pipeline used to convert prompts and environment snapshots into validated scenes and executable task programs. Unless noted otherwise, experiments use a 16k/2k/2k split for train/validation/test; asset and task program splits are detailed in the Appendix. All scenes are fully navigable and support interaction and manipulation without additional authoring. In scale and interactivity, EnvDreamer–20k is among the largest public corpora designed for embodied AI, world models, and robotics. Scaling beyond 20k is straightforward because each scene is specified by a compact program with deterministic seeds and can be generated on demand during training. Figure 4 shows ego centric and top down views. 

Scene statistics. EnvDreamer–20k currently focuses on indoor environments such as single rooms, multi-room apartments, and offices. We control distributions over room count and connectivity via high-level specifications, and empirical statistics are reported in Figure 6. Across the dataset, most environments contain 3–6 rooms, with smaller studio-like scenes and larger multi-room layouts appearing less frequently. Within each room, object counts follow a controlled distribution centered around 10–16 items, with occasional clutter-heavy rooms containing up to 30 objects. Materials, textures, and lighting vary across loads to simulate different times of day and appearance conditions. 

Tasks and validators. Each scene includes one or more executable task programs with explicit preconditions and success predicates. Tasks span object search, waypoint navigation, pick and place and rearrangement, tool use, and multi step manipulation. Unit style validators check playability, physics consistency, and goal completeness before inclusion. We report task counts and pass rates per family in Figure 6b and provide failure taxonomies in the Appendix. 

Release format. Each entry in EnvDreamer–20k includes: the original prompt or authoring images, the scene program, the scene graph, rendering metadata, task programs and validator outcomes, and optional reference trajectories. We also provide seed files for exact regeneration and scripts for batch materialization inside Unreal Engine 5. This format reduces setup cost and supports reproducible benchmarking for world models, embodied agents, and robotics. 

# 5 Experiments 

We empirically evaluate EnvDreamer–20k on navigation and manipulation benchmarks that mirror the ProcTHOR evaluation protocol. Concretely, we focus on ObjectNav-style navigation in several simulators and on two manipulation-style tasks: ArmPointNav and one-phase Room Rearrangement. This allows us to directly compare pretraining on EnvDreamer–20k to pretraining on ProcTHOR-10K using the same task definitions and metrics. 

5.1 Tasks and Benchmarks Navigation. We consider ObjectNav (navigation towards a specific object category) in five bench-marks: ProcTHOR-style houses, ArchitecTHOR, RoboTHOR, HM3D, and AI2-iTHOR. Episodes begin from a random spawn within the environment and specify a target object category. The agent receives egocentric RGB observations and must navigate to within a small distance of an instance of the target category while avoiding collisions. Following standard practice, we report success rate (SR), success weighted by path length (SPL) Anderson et al. [2018].

5.2 Comparison to ProcTHOR 

For fair comparison, all models use identical simple RGB-only architectures without explicit mapping or depth sensing. We evaluate in two modes: zero-shot (pretrained only, no downstream training) and fine-tuning (pretrained then adapted on benchmark data). ProcTHOR-10K demonstrated that large-scale procedural houses plus a simple RGB-only model can achieve state-of-the-art results across six embodied benchmarks. EnvDreamer-20k follows the same philosophy but is built in Unreal Engine 5 and tightly integrates language-driven generation, task programs, and validator checks. # 6 Discussion and Limitations 

While EnvDreamer-20k advances the scale of interactive embodied AI environments, several limita-tions suggest future directions. 

Scope and coverage. EnvDreamer-20k currently focuses on indoor environments; outdoor scenes are supported by the framework and will be included in future releases. The system lacks complex physics (liquids, deformables) and supports only single-agent tasks. Generation takes 2–5 minutes per environment, and CPU bottlenecks reduce per-instance throughput at high parallelism, though aggregate performance suffices for training. 

Task complexity. Tasks are limited to relatively short horizons (10–50 steps) with single solutions. Future work will extend to longer-horizon, open-ended specifications with multiple valid approaches that better reflect real-world complexity. 

Future directions. Key extensions include outdoor environments, advanced physics (liquids, deformables), multi-agent validators, curriculum learning, longer-horizon tasks with multiple solution paths, and a pipeline under development for exporting scenes to NVIDIA Omniverse for use within Isaac Sim and the broader Omniverse robotics tooling. The open-source release enables community extensions to assets, tasks, and validators. Despite these limitations, EnvDreamer-20k demonstrates that language-driven, validator-checked generation produces diverse, high-quality training data at scale, achieving competitive performance across benchmarks while maintaining rapid iteration capabilities. 

# 7 Conclusion 

We presented EnvDreamer, a language-first framework for generating arbitrarily large sets of interac-tive, physics-enabled Unreal Engine 5 environments for embodied AI, world models, and robotics. EnvDreamer-20k—our dataset of 20,000 validated environments with executable task programs, scene graphs, and metadata—enables policies that consistently outperform ProcTHOR-10K pretrain-ing across all tested navigation, rearrangement, and manipulation benchmarks, with strong zero-shot transfer capabilities. Scaling experiments confirm that performance continues to improve with dataset size, validating the investment in large-scale generation. The open-source release of EnvDreamer and the full EnvDreamer-20k dataset aims to democratize access to high-quality training environments and accelerate progress in embodied AI research. By bridging vision-language models with physics simulation and unit-style validation, EnvDreamer provides a scalable, reproducible foundation for embodied intelligence. 9References

8.1 EnvDreamer Remote Control API 

EnvDreamer exposes a lightweight Remote Control (RC) API that allows Python tools and external agents to programmatically construct, inspect, and manipulate scenes inside Unreal Engine. The API is implemented by the AJsonSceneBuilder actor on the engine side and a small set of client utilities on the Python side (e.g., rc_call() in HomeAgent ). This section summarizes the design and typical usage patterns of the API. 

8.1.1 JsonSceneBuilder Actor 

The AJsonSceneBuilder actor is responsible for: • maintaining a map from user-level identifiers ( Id ) to spawned actors using tags of the form 

"RCID:<Id>" ,• interpreting RC commands (add/remove assets, set transforms, apply materials/textures), and • exposing camera and snapshot utilities for debugging and data collection. Existing actors marked as "JsonGenerated" are re-indexed at startup via 

ReindexExistingJsonActors() , allowing scenes built in previous sessions to be reused without re-spawning from scratch. 

8.1.2 Asset Resolution and Registry 

To decouple layout descriptions from engine paths, EnvDreamer uses a simple asset registry: • Config file. At editor startup, AssetRegistry::Initialize() loads 

Config/asset_registry.json , which stores assets grouped by semantic categories (e.g., "kitchen" , "office" ) and an optional alias map. • Name resolution. AssetRegistry::Resolve(NameOrPath) returns a fully qualified Un-real path when given a shortcut name (e.g., "bp_table" ) or passes through raw /Game/... 

paths unchanged. • Type detection. RC_AddAsset uses Unreal’s IAssetRegistry to distinguish between static mesh assets and Blueprint classes, falling back to "_C" suffix heuristics when metadata is unavailable. 12 Table 4: Key EnvDreamer RC methods callable via Python.

8.2 Remote Unreal Web Session 

To make EnvDreamer accessible without opening the Unreal Editor, a minimal “simple builder” web session is provided. It couples a Pixel Streaming viewport running inside UE5 with a small FastAPI backend and a single-page HTML/JavaScript client. This interface is used for quick qualitative inspection, debugging, and human-in-the-loop layout editing. 

8.2.1 Pixel-Streamed Viewer 

On the engine side, Unreal’s Pixel Streaming stack exposes the current editor or game viewport as a low-latency WebRTC stream. The web client embeds this stream in an <iframe> (pointing to the local Pixel Streaming address) and forwards mouse and keyboard events directly to UE5. This allows a remote user to orbit, pan, and fly through the generated environment and visually confirm that RC-issued layout changes have taken effect. No simulation logic runs in the browser; the page is a thin remote viewer and input forwarder. 14 8.2.2 Simple Builder Server 

The simple builder backend is a FastAPI application that exposes a small set of HTTP endpoints: • GET / serves the static HTML page ( web_simple.html ) containing the viewer and chat-style layout assistant. • GET /shots/latest.png returns the latest high-resolution screenshot produced by UE5. Screenshots are copied into a public directory and served with no-cache headers. • POST /api/simple/build-from-prompt accepts a JSON payload with a free-form text description, calls generate_layout_from_prompt() , writes the resulting layout JSON, and invokes apply_layout_update() to rebuild the scene. • POST /api/simple/build-from-image accepts an uploaded reference image plus an optional text prompt, calls generate_layout_from_image() , and applies the resulting layout in the same way as the text-only endpoint. Both build endpoints return a compact update summary describing the change mode (full vs. in-cremental), which rooms were touched, and which items were updated. The web client uses this summary to render concise status messages in the chat log. 

8.2.3 Browser UI and Interaction Loop 

The HTML client exposes two main panels: a viewer panel with the embedded Pixel Streaming iframe, and a “Layout Assistant” panel that implements a chat-style interface. Each user request is echoed as a user message, and each successful build is summarized as an assistant message. Drag-and-drop handlers allow a user to paste or drop an image directly into the chat area, which triggers the image-conditioned endpoint. The JavaScript logic maintains a small amount of local state, including a reference count of active HTTP requests (used to disable the send button and prevent overlapping builds) and error handling that surfaces non-2xx responses as concise error messages in the chat log. Layout state is not cached in the browser; the authoritative layout and history live on disk and are queried when needed. 

8.2.4 Layout History and Checkpoints 

To support backtracking and side-by-side comparisons, the simple builder records layout checkpoints under Config/LayoutHistory/ . Each successful build creates a JSON snapshot and appends a version entry to history.json with a timestamp, source type (prompt, image, clear, etc.), and an optional note. The browser queries GET /api/simple/layout/state , which returns the current layout, the list of versions, and the identifier of the active checkpoint; this list populates the “Layout History” sidebar with simple “Previous”, “Next”, and “Restore” controls. Dedicated endpoints (/api/simple/layout/restore , /api/simple/layout/clear ,

/api/simple/layout/set-wall-color ) allow a user to restore a previous snapshot, clear the layout, or apply targeted edits (such as changing a room’s wall color) without re-running the planner. Restoring a checkpoint updates the default layout file, invokes apply_layout_update() 

to resynchronize UE5, and marks the selected version as current in history.json .

8.2.5 Intended Usage 

The remote web session is intended for rapid manual exploration and debugging rather than large-scale training. Typical uses include: interactive testing of planner behaviour without opening the Unreal Editor, quick qualitative evaluation of layout diversity and failure modes by non-expert users, and easy reproduction of specific layouts from the paper by loading the corresponding checkpoint and streaming the resulting scene. Because the interface is built entirely on top of the RC API and planner endpoints, it can be deployed on remote hosts or extended with task-specific controls without changes to the core engine. 

8.3 Layout Planner and Prompt Design 

The main paper describes the overall EnvDreamer pipeline at a high level. Here we focus on the layout planner component that translates text (and optionally images) into editable layouts. 15 Figure 9: High-level control flow of the layout planner. 

8.3.1 Planner API and Control Flow 

The planner exposes a small API used by the UE5 tools: • generate_layout_from_prompt(prompt, current_layout) produces a full layout JSON given a natural-language instruction and, optionally, a pre-existing layout to edit. • generate_layout_from_image(prompt, image_bytes, current_layout) recon-structs a layout from a reference image plus optional text. • validate_layout_schema(layout) and write_layout_file(path, layout) pro-vide lightweight validation and serialization. Internally, both generation functions construct a list of messages that combine: (1) a global system prompt describing geometric and semantic constraints; (2) an optional summary of the current layout (when editing); and (3) a strict JSON schema specification that pins down the output format. The user instruction is appended as the final user message. We request a structured response via 

response_format={ "type": "json_object" } , which avoids the need for ad-hoc text post-processing. 

8.3.2 Incremental Editing Behaviour 

In interactive sessions the user rarely specifies an entire apartment in one prompt. Instead, EnvDreamer receives a sequence of localized edits: “extend the kitchen by one grid cell,” “replace the sofa with two arm chairs,” or “turn the study into a bedroom.” To support these workflows: • The planner always passes the most recent layout as an additional system message tagged as current_layout . The prompt emphasizes that unspecified rooms and items should be preserved. • When the user request is ambiguous (e.g., “make the living room brighter”), we treat the instruction as a soft preference and allow the LLM to choose between adjusting lighting, swapping materials, or both. The resulting layout is still filtered by the validator. • If schema validation fails, the tool surfaces a concise error message and offers to either (i) re-issue the prompt with a stronger constraint hint, or (ii) revert to the previous layout. 

8.3.3 Vision-conditioned Layouts 

For image-conditioned generation, we embed the input photograph as an image_url in the user 

message and prepend a short instruction such as “ Infer a plausible floor plan for this room ” or “ Align the furniture arrangement with this photo while keeping the existing topology .” Unlike the text-only 16 Figure 10: Illustrative incremental editing session. 

Figure 11: Distribution of room counts and usable floor area. case, the planner treats the image as soft evidence: it aims to match visible structure and object types while still respecting grid snapping and validator constraints. We found that allowing the model to slightly deviate from the exact pixel geometry leads to layouts that are easier to navigate and reuse across tasks, at the cost of a small loss in photometric fidelity.

8.6 Training and Evaluation Protocol 

The main paper reports the high-level RL setup. Here we add details that are mainly of interest for reproduction and extension. 

8.6.1 Compute Budget and Parallelism 

All pretraining and evaluation runs reported in the main paper were executed on a small cluster of NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs using synchronous PPO. Environments are stepped in parallel across 16–32 workers, with each worker hosting multiple UE5 instances where hardware permits. We cap the wall-clock time per update to avoid stragglers; experience from slow workers in a given round is discarded rather than delaying gradient updates. 

Rendering and throughput. EnvDreamer instances are capped at 120 FPS per environment to maintain stable physics simulation. While a single GPU efficiently runs 3 parallel instances at full framerate, scaling to multiple GPUs introduces CPU bottlenecks that reduce per-instance throughput. Despite these limitations, the system achieves aggregate framerates sufficient for large-scale training. Table 6 reports measured throughput across configurations.

8.7.4 Validator Strictness

8.9 House Building Engine 

The layout planner produces a JSON description of rooms, bounds, and materials. The HomeAgent 

module is responsible for compiling this description into a concrete UE5 scene by instantiating parameterized primitives, assigning textures, and inserting door transitions. 

8.9.1 Texture Registry and Material Shortcuts 

To keep layouts compact and decoupled from engine paths, HomeAgent uses a small texture reg-istry . At import time, _load_texture_registry() reads Config/texture_registry.json 

and caches a mapping from user-friendly shortcuts (e.g., "wood_light" , "concrete" ) to full Unreal material paths: • The registry is loaded once at module level into _TEXTURE_REGISTRY to avoid repeated I/O. • _get_texture_path(name) resolves a shortcut to a full path, or returns the input un-changed if no mapping is found. This makes the system robust to both abstract labels and explicit engine paths. 22 Figure 17: Overview of the house building engine implemented by HomeAgent .Textures are applied lazily: after a cube is spawned, apply_texture(id, texture_name) issues an RC_SetMaterialTexture call with the resolved material path. When a texture is present, the agent intentionally omits per-vertex color parameters so that materials control appearance consistently. 

8.9.2 Primitive-Based Geometry 

The engine builds environments entirely from a single cube asset, scaled and positioned to realize floors and walls: • Tile and pivot. The cube is parameterized in units of a configurable tile size (default 100 cm). A global pivot mode ( "base" or "center" ) controls whether cubes are anchored at their bottom face or at their center; this is handled by _cube_z(height, bottom_z) .• Spawn API. spawn_cube(id, x, y, z, sx, sy, sz, color) wraps a single call to 

RC_AddAsset with position, scale, and orientation. Colors are passed only when no texture is specified, mirroring the schema convention that color and texture are mutually exclusive. • Global parameters. Scene-wide quantities such as wall thickness, floor thickness, door width, and building boundaries are read from the layout file, allowing different layouts to share the same builder logic. 

8.9.3 Wall Construction and Door Carving 

Walls are constructed by scanning each room and each side of its bounding box. Let name be the current room and side ∈ { north , south , east , west }.1. Adjacency detection. _adjacent_room_on_side(name, side) searches for a neigh-boring room whose bounds touch the current side along one axis and overlap along the orthogonal axis. If no neighbor exists, the side is exposed to the building boundary. 2. Ownership and door span. To avoid double walls, a simple lexical tie-break 

_owns_shared_wall(a,b) chooses which room is responsible for a shared wall. If name 

owns the wall, _door_span(name, other, side) computes a candidate door interval along the shared segment using the configured door width. 3. Segment splitting. The helper spawn_segments_for_side decomposes the full wall segment into: • solo segments with no neighbor (external walls), • shared segments without doors, and • shared segments with a carved-out door interval. Each non-empty segment is realized as a scaled cube with the appropriate wall color or texture. 4. Door floor patches. When a door is carved between two rooms, the agent records two small floor patches—one for each room—with their respective floor colors or tex-tures. These patches are not spawned immediately; instead they are accumulated in 

door_floor_patches and instantiated in the floor pass, ensuring consistent layering. 23 Figure 18: Wall segmentation and door carving, including door floor patches. 

8.9.4 Floor Slabs and Insets 

Floors are generated in a second pass via build_all_floors() :• For each room, the builder computes an inner rectangle obtained by insetting the bounds by half the wall thickness. This prevents floors from protruding through the walls while keeping a clean visual seam. • If the inset would collapse the interior (e.g., extremely thin rooms), the code gracefully falls back to using the full bounds. • A single cube is spawned per room to represent its floor slab, with either a solid color or a texture, matching the layout’s floor_color / floor_texture fields. • Door floor patches recorded during wall construction are then instantiated as additional thin slabs, using per-room floor settings. This produces visually continuous transitions between rooms while preserving room-specific appearances. The result is a watertight arrangement of walls and floors that strictly respects the planner’s bounds and adjacency relationships, while remaining easy to edit: small changes in the JSON layout (e.g., moving a wall by one grid cell) directly translate to different cube placements. 

8.9.5 Agent Interface and Entry Points 

The public entry point setup_home() exposes a simple interface for downstream tools and scripts: • The layout argument selects between a default layout file, a small test layout, or an arbitrary user-provided JSON. • The room argument enables partial builds (e.g., to rapidly iterate on a single room without rebuilding the whole apartment). 24 • The delay and pivot parameters control spawn pacing (useful for debugging) and cube pivot mode, respectively. The HomeAgent.clear_home() and move_camera() helpers are used by interactive front-ends to reset the scene and position the camera before and after a build, but do not affect the underlying layout. Overall, this design allows EnvDreamer to compile high-level, LLM-generated layouts into con-crete UE5 scenes using a small, deterministic Python module, while keeping textures and assets configurable via external registries. 25 NeurIPS Paper Checklist
